Privacy policy

Dust — CW Morse trainer
Android package dev.dust.morse
Effective date: 30 October 2026

This policy covers the Dust app on Android and iPhone, and the same program on the web at mashu.github.io/dust and on desktop: Linux, Windows and macOS. It describes what the app stores, where, what leaves the device, and who receives it.

The short version

Dust stores your practice on the device you are using. It has no account and no server of its own, and it never sends your practice anywhere. The app asks Google for its typefaces, and the web app is served by GitHub. A phone’s own backup, when it is on, copies the app’s data to your Google or iCloud account, as it does for other apps. In a browser, the other sites the developer publishes at mashu.github.io share the storage Dust keeps it in.

Who publishes Dust

Dust is an open-source Morse trainer. The source and this policy are published at github.com/mashu/dust. There is no account system, and the developer does not receive your practice history.

What the app stores on your device

Dust stores the following on the device so practice can continue the next time you open it:

  • Settings. Speed, character set and any word list you add to it, session length, keyer, tone, band conditions, theme, and whether the guided setup has been opened, so it opens by itself only the first time. In Contest mode, also your callsign and the exchange you send.
  • Practice history. For each session: the date and time, the groups that were sent, the characters you typed and how soon after each character's sound you keyed it, with the delay your audio device reports, accuracy, scores, speed, and per-letter results. For a contest run, also its log of QSOs and your country, continent and CQ zone. Up to 500 sessions are kept; in a browser, as many of the newest as fit.
  • Level counters. Counts used to decide when to raise or lower the level you are practising.
  • A session in progress. While a session or contest run is running, when it started and what it has done so far: the groups answered, with the same details the practice history keeps, and the settings the session runs on, or for a contest run its log of the QSOs worked. If the app closes under it, Dust keeps that part in the practice history the next time it opens, and says so. It is removed when the session ends, or once it has been kept.

In Contest mode, Dust keeps your callsign and the exchange you send (name, state or province, CQ zone, class, section, power) in its settings on this device. Each contest run keeps only your country, continent and CQ zone, so its score can be worked out again. Both are in any backup you save. Dust never sends them anywhere. Clear Your station to remove them. The stations you work are generated by Dust; if one matches a real station, that is chance.

Dust does not use any of it for advertising, and it does not send it to the developer. Where it is kept, and what else may keep a copy, depends on where you run Dust:

  • On Android all of this is kept in storage that only the app can read. If backup is on in Android settings, Android copies it to your Google account, as it does for most apps, and puts it back on a new phone or when Dust is installed again. Uninstalling the app deletes it from the phone, but not that copy. Turning backup off in Android settings stops it. This is Android’s own backup, not a feature of Dust: the copy is in your Google account, the developer never receives it, and Google holds it under Google’s privacy policy. Setting up a new phone straight from the old one can copy it across too. Turning off backup in Android settings stops the copy to your Google account, for every app at once.
  • On an iPhone all of this is kept in storage that only the app can read. iCloud Backup, and a backup of the iPhone on a computer, copy it as they do for other apps, and restoring such a backup puts it back. Deleting the app deletes it from the iPhone, but not those copies. Those are Apple’s backups, not a feature of Dust, and the developer never receives them. In the iPhone’s iCloud settings, iCloud Backup can be turned off for Dust alone.
  • In a browser all of this is kept in the storage of the site mashu.github.io, which every other site the developer publishes there shares: such a site could read it or clear it. Clearing the site data for mashu.github.io deletes it, along with what those sites keep. A browser keeps storage for a whole site, not for each page under it, and every project the developer publishes on GitHub Pages is a page under mashu.github.io. Only the developer’s GitHub repositories can publish there, and whatever a page there loads shares its access. Dust keeps everything under names that begin with dust_, and never touches anything else the site keeps. The room the browser gives the site is shared too: where other pages use some of it, the history keeps fewer of its newest sessions, and the app says so. The app asks the browser to keep this storage rather than clear it to free space; the browser may ask you first. Nothing is synced between browsers or devices.
  • On Linux all of this is kept in ~/.local/share/dust. Uninstalling the app leaves that folder: delete it to delete what Dust stored. A backup of your home folder copies it. Where $XDG_DATA_HOME is set, the folder is $XDG_DATA_HOME/dust instead.
  • On Windows all of this is kept in %APPDATA%\dust. Uninstalling the app leaves that folder: delete it to delete what Dust stored. A backup of your user folder copies it.
  • On a Mac all of this is kept in ~/Library/Application Support/dust. Deleting the app leaves that folder: delete it to delete what Dust stored. A backup of your home folder, such as Time Machine makes, copies it.

Dust itself syncs nothing between devices: it has no server to sync through. A backup file, below, is how to move your history yourself.

Backups. Under Stats, History, you can save a backup file of your settings and history. The web app hands it to the browser as a download; desktop builds and the Android app put it in your Downloads folder; the iPhone app puts it in its own folder in the Files app. The app never sends it anywhere. Restoring a backup reads the file you choose on the device and adds what it holds to the history there.

How that information is used

It is used only to run the trainer: to play sessions at the settings you chose, to score what you typed, to show your history and statistics, and to adjust the level when you have turned that on. It is not used to identify you, and it is not combined with anyone else’s data.

What leaves the device

Dust does not include advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting software. It does not ask you to sign in.

The app does request its typefaces from Google Fonts (fonts.googleapis.com and fonts.gstatic.com). That request includes your IP address and the ordinary technical data a connection carries, such as the browser or web-view identifier. Your settings and practice history are not included. Google receives that request and handles it under Google’s privacy policy. On Android, iPhone and desktop it is the only request the app makes.

The web app is served by GitHub Pages: the page, the program and its styles are loaded from GitHub each time you open it. That sends GitHub your IP address and the same technical data, as opening any web page does, and GitHub keeps a record of the visit under the GitHub General Privacy Statement. Your settings and practice history are not part of it. Google and GitHub are the only third parties the web app contacts.

A phone’s own backup, described above, is made by Android or iOS, not by the app, and goes to your own Google or iCloud account.

If you install Dust from Google Play or the App Store, Google or Apple also handles the download and the store listing under its own policies; if you download it from GitHub, GitHub does. That collection is theirs, not a feature of the app.

Microphone, location, and other sensitive data

Dust plays sound through the speaker or headphones. It does not record the microphone. Paddle practice uses the keyboard or on-screen buttons.

Dust does not access your location, contacts, photos, or messages. It reads a file only when you choose one — a word list, or a backup to restore — and writes one only when you save a backup. It does not collect payment information. There are no purchases in the app.

Children

Dust is a general-audience practice tool. It is not directed at children, and it does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

How long it is kept, and how to delete it

The history stays on the device until you remove it. Up to 500 sessions are kept; in a browser, as many of the newest as fit. Older ones are discarded as you practise. On every platform any session, or all of them, can be deleted under Stats, History, and clearing Your station removes your callsign and exchange.

  • Android. Uninstall Dust, or clear the app’s storage in system settings. That deletes the settings and the history from the phone. A copy Android has backed up stays in your Google account, and comes back if Dust is installed again while backup is on; turn backup off first to keep it from coming back, or delete the phone’s backup from your Google account.
  • iPhone. Delete the app. That deletes the settings and the history from the iPhone. A backup of the iPhone made before then still holds them, and restoring it brings them back.
  • Web. Delete all the sessions under Stats, History, and clear Your station. That leaves the practice settings, the app’s own counters and marks, none of which say who you are, and any copy of a history Dust could not read and set aside, and touches no other site. Clearing the site data for https://mashu.github.io in your browser’s settings deletes everything Dust keeps there, along with what any other site at mashu.github.io keeps.
  • Desktop. Delete the dust folder named above: ~/.local/share/dust on Linux, %APPDATA%\dust on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/dust on macOS. Uninstalling the app does not delete it.

A backup file you saved is yours, and is deleted like any other file. There is no account to delete, because the app never receives a copy.

Sharing and sale

Dust does not sell personal information. It does not share practice history with anyone. The app sends data to no one but Google, and only the font request described above; the web app is loaded from GitHub. Dust does not share data with advertising networks or data brokers.
